## Perfect Store Prioritization: Unlocking Untapped Potential Across India

To our Field Sales Leadership Team,

This Perfect Store prioritization analysis provides a data-driven roadmap to optimize our Go-To-Market efforts, ensuring we focus our resources where they yield the highest commercial returns. By segmenting our 10,000 retail outlets across India and identifying their true potential, we can move beyond intuition-led execution to a scalable, data-backed strategy.

Our quantile regression model, which predicts the 75th percentile VPO (Value Per Outlet), clearly highlights the environmental factors that most strongly drive an outlet's revenue potential in the Indian FMCG landscape. The top drivers are consistently **neighbourhood demographics (per-capita income, SEC household distribution), store size, and proximity to commercial hubs** such as offices, malls, schools, and transport stations. This confirms that a store's inherent potential is deeply tied to its catchment's affluence and the footfall generated by its surroundings. For territory planning, this means prioritizing areas with higher SEC households and strong commercial activity, ensuring our sales force is strategically deployed to geographies with the highest inherent VPO potential. It validates our focus on quality of catchment over mere quantity of outlets, enabling differentiated service models for maximum impact.

### Prioritizing High-Impact Interventions: A+ and B+ Stores

Our analysis reveals that **A+ (6.4% of outlets)** and **B+ (14.6% of outlets)** stores represent our highest-priority intervention targets. These outlets are already strong performers, ranking in the top 40% by actual VPO, yet they exhibit significant untapped potential. An A+ store, for instance, generates an average VPO of ₹6,325 but could achieve ₹7,917, leaving a ₹1,592 gap. Similarly, B+ stores, with an average VPO of ₹3,384, have a substantial ₹1,789 gap against their predicted potential of ₹5,173.

The fact that these stores are already high-revenue but still below their environmental potential indicates they are leaving significant money on the table. This isn't a problem of poor location or lack of shoppers; rather, it suggests sub-optimal execution within a highly favorable environment. Interventions here are likely to yield the fastest and highest ROI. The gap can typically be closed by:

*   **Execution Upgrade & Premiumization (A+):** Optimizing planograms for high-margin SKUs, ensuring full portfolio availability, prioritizing new product launches (NPDs), installing/upgrading chillers, and implementing premium merchandising (MBD) to enhance shopper experience and drive basket size.
*   **Distribution Fill & Visibility Boost (B+):** Aggressively pushing for MSL compliance, ensuring availability of hero SKUs, deploying secondary displays for impulse purchases, and enhancing visibility with impactful POSM.

### Strategic Approaches for C+ and D+ Stores

The **C+ (25.7% of outlets)** and **D+ (28.2% of outlets)** segments also show significant positive opportunity gaps (₹1,589 and ₹1,316 respectively). These are not structural low-performers; rather, they are outlets in decent catchments that are currently underperforming their environment. This suggests they are either underserved by our current GTM model or are not optimizing their sales.

Our approach to these segments must be different from A+/B+. While A+/B+ focus on optimizing existing strong performance, C+/D+ require building foundational performance. Investment here needs to be more strategic and potentially phased:

*   **C+ (Strategic Investment):** These mid-revenue stores in favorable catchments warrant strategic investment. This includes implementing cooler seeding programs where justified, driving MSL compliance, providing basic POSM, and increasing visit frequency to build rapport and educate retailers on category growth. The goal is to unlock latent potential through targeted, foundational investment.
*   **D+ (Trial Activation):** These low-revenue stores with potential require a more cautious, trial-based approach. We should introduce affordable entry-pack SKUs (e.g., ₹5/₹10 packs), conduct basic awareness drives, and explore low-cost service models. Performance must be monitored closely to validate the potential before committing to further significant investment.

### Recommended Field Action Matrix

To translate this analysis into actionable steps for our field teams, here is a prioritized action matrix:

*   **A+ (Top 20% VPO, Underperforming Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Execution Upgrade & Premiumization.** Optimize planograms for high-margin SKUs, ensure full portfolio availability, prioritize new product launches (NPDs), install/upgrade chillers, implement premium merchandising (MBD). Focus on increasing basket size and conversion.
*   **A (Top 20% VPO, At Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Protect & Retain.** Maintain strong trade terms, ensure loyalty programs are active, prioritize first-mover advantage on NPDs, monitor competitor activity closely. Focus on sustaining high performance and preventing erosion.
*   **B+ (Next 20% VPO, Underperforming Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Distribution Fill & Visibility Boost.** Aggressively push for MSL compliance, ensure availability of hero SKUs, deploy secondary displays for impulse purchases, enhance visibility with impactful POSM. Focus on improving fundamental availability and shopper engagement.
*   **B (Next 20% VPO, At Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Maintain Frequency & Seasonal Activation.** Ensure consistent visit cadence, execute seasonal promotions and activations effectively, maintain strong retailer relationships. Focus on steady performance and leveraging tactical opportunities.
*   **C+ (Next 30% VPO, Underperforming Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Strategic Investment & Foundational Build.** Implement cooler seeding programs where justified, drive MSL compliance, provide basic POSM, and increase visit frequency to build rapport and educate retailers on category growth. Focus on unlocking latent potential through targeted investment.
*   **C (Next 30% VPO, At Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Efficiency & Digital Enablement.** Optimize visit routes to reduce cost-to-serve, encourage digital reordering platforms, focus on essential MSL maintenance. Focus on profitable service models.
*   **D+ (Bottom 30% VPO, Underperforming Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Trial Activation & Entry-Pack Introduction.** Introduce affordable entry-pack SKUs (e.g., ₹5/₹10 packs), conduct basic awareness drives, and explore low-cost service models. Monitor performance closely to validate potential before further investment.
*   **D (Bottom 30% VPO, At Potential):**
    *   **Action:** **Rationalize & Lowest-Cost Service.** Reduce visit frequency, explore alternative service models (e.g., tele-sales, distributor-led), focus on essential product availability. Prioritize efficiency and minimize cost-to-serve for these structural low-performers.

By adopting this data-driven prioritization and action framework, we can ensure our Perfect Store initiatives are commercially grounded, driving sustainable revenue and margin growth across our diverse retail landscape.
